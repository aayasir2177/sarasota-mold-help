Why Mold Is So Common in Sarasota County Homes

Mold needs moisture, and the Gulf Coast supplies plenty of it. The air outside is damp nearly every month, summer afternoons bring heavy rain, and air conditioners run almost nonstop. Your AC is the main thing standing between that damp air and the paper face of your drywall. When a condensate line clogs or a duct sweats in a hot attic, growth follows quickly.

Storms add to the problem. Hurricane Ian flooded neighborhoods in North Port in 2022. In late September 2024, Helene pushed surge into Englewood and onto the keys. Milton followed on October 9, coming ashore near Siesta Key. Walls that looked dry after the cleanup can still hold moisture behind the paint.

Then there's the housing itself: mid-century block homes with original windows, condo towers where one unit's leak turns into a neighbor's ceiling stain, newer two-story homes with an air handler in the attic, and seasonal homes that sit closed for months with the AC turned up.

Ceiling Mold Removal

Questions to Ask Before Work Starts

In Florida, the people who assess mold and the people who remove it hold different licenses. Four questions keep any job on track:

  • Can I have your Florida license number? You can verify it in a minute on myfloridalicense.com.
  • Is the water source fixed, or scheduled to be fixed, before removal starts?
  • Does the written plan list every material coming out, what gets cleaned in place, and how the work area is sealed off?
  • When the removal is finished, who signs off on it? Ideally that's a separate company from the one doing the work.

Common Questions

Can I clean mold myself?

A small patch on tile, a tub, or a window sill is usually a household cleaning job. Growth on drywall, wood, or ceilings, anything inside the AC system, and anything that follows a flood is work for a professional. The EPA's rule of thumb is about 10 square feet: if it's bigger than that, call a pro.

Do I need a mold inspection or a mold test?

An inspection finds the moisture and shows how big the problem is. A test answers a narrower question with lab results. Most homes with a smell or a stain start with an inspection, and sampling gets added when it answers something specific. The guide on mold inspection vs. mold testing explains the difference.

Does homeowners insurance pay for mold in Florida?

Sometimes. Mold from a sudden covered event, like a burst supply line, is more likely to be covered than mold from a slow leak, and many policies cap what they pay for mold. Flood damage needs a separate flood policy. Check your declarations page and talk to your carrier early. This is general information, not insurance advice.

How fast does mold grow after water gets in?

On wet drywall, carpet, and wood, mold can start within about 24 to 48 hours, according to the EPA. Getting materials dry quickly matters more than any spray or cleaner you can buy.
